Learn Notion with Phoebe

A Notion workspace is a small database you live inside, and it stays useful only while each fact in it is written down once. Six sessions build one working life-and-work system: pages against databases, properties chosen by the question they answer, relations and rollups so a client's name and rate live in one row, formulas, templates and buttons that do the typing for you, and a dashboard you open every Monday. One constructed running case, Ines, a freelance user researcher, and one bench that counts, in your browser, what copied text does to her workspace after a few ordinary edits.

The six sessions 🧭 · from a blank page to the Monday dashboard

The running case is Ines, a constructed freelance user-research consultant. She has four active clients and one part-time contractor, Theo, and her operations live in five places at once: a notes page per client, a task app, a spreadsheet of invoices, her calendar and her inbox. Across six sessions she moves them into one Notion workspace: clients, projects, tasks, meetings, invoices, a reading list and a weekly dashboard. Ines, Theo and every client are invented for teaching, and every page says so. Notion ships features every month, so each page names only what its help pages say today.

📄 Session 1 · foundations

Pages are not databases

A page holds writing; a database holds things you will ask questions about, and every row in it is itself a page. Inline against full-page, data sources, views as lenses, and Ines's first skeleton.

▶ Start here 🏷️ Session 2 · core

Properties that answer questions

Choose each property by the question you will ask of it. Status against select, dates, created and edited time, and views with filters, sorts and groups saved for everyone.

types and views 🔗 Session 3 · core

Relations and rollups

One fact, one place: Codd's old idea in a modern tool. One-way and two-way relations, the one-page limit, and the rollup calculations that count, sum and date what a relation reaches.

one fact, one place 🧮 Session 4 · bench night

The drift bench

Ines's constructed workspace, two designs, six ordinary edits. Count the rows that now disagree, the invoices billed at a stale rate and the questions answered wrong, then try one giant table and delete a client.

★ the bench ⚙️ Session 5 · deeper

Formulas, templates and buttons

Formulas 2.0 syntax read at source, an invoice that works out its own amount, templates that repeat, and a button that freezes a rate at the moment an invoice is issued.

let the tool type 📅 Session 6 · deeper

The weekly dashboard you open every Monday

Linked views on one page, each filtered for one purpose, who can edit what when Theo joins, and the fifteen-minute Monday ritual that keeps the workspace honest.

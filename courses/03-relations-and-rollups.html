Relations and rollups: one fact, one place

Part 1 · covers the one-fact-one-place rule

Every copy is a promise to update it

5 min live

In 1970, long before anyone typed a client list into Notion, E. F. Codd wrote about exactly this trap. His relational model paper applies operations on relations "to the problems of redundancy and consistency". The everyday version: write a fact twice and, sooner or later, the two copies disagree. The textbook form of his idea is "one fact in one place". That phrase is the teaching shorthand, not his words.

LiveCodd's problem in a client list3 min▶

Codd's paper calls a set of relations redundant when one of them holds something that can be derived from the others. Ines's rate on a project row is exactly that: it can be derived from the client the project belongs to. Derivable facts written down again are where trouble starts, because each copy has to be updated separately.

His second idea is the sharper one for working life. Consistency, he wrote, is "a property of the instantaneous state of a data bank", "independent of how that state came about". Put plainly: when the project row says 125 and the client page says 140, it does not matter whether Ines mistyped or forgot. The workspace is wrong either way, and a report built on it is wrong either way. You cannot fix that with care; you fix it by having only one place for the rate to be.

Self-studyWhere normal forms live2 min▶

The formal machinery that grew out of Codd's work, the normal forms and the update anomalies they prevent, came in his later papers and belongs to schema design. It is taught properly, with an anomaly lab, in Data Modeling ↗. Here you only need the working rule: before you add a field, ask whether that fact is already written somewhere else. If it is, point at it with a relation instead of typing it again.

Real world

The email address that changed in one tool. A familiar pattern in small teams: a client contact changes jobs and sends a new email address. Someone updates the contact list, nobody updates the mailing tool, and the next three updates go to a dead inbox. No one made a mistake on the day. The mistake was keeping the address in two places.

Part 2 · covers relations

Point at the client, never retype it

5 min live

A relation is a property whose value is another page. Instead of typing "Brightwell Mutual" into a project, Ines links the project to the Brightwell Mutual row in Clients. Rename the client once and every linked project shows the new name, because the project never held a copy of the name; it held a pointer to the row.

LiveOne-way, two-way, and Show on3 min▶

Notion's relations help page is precise about the default: a relation between two databases is created one-way. Projects gets a Client property, and Clients gets nothing. Turn on the "Show on [database]" toggle, here "Show on Clients", and Notion adds the matching property in the other database, so Brightwell Mutual's page now lists its projects. Ines wants that: opening a client and seeing everything running for them is half the point.

The second setting is the limit: 1 page or No limit. A project has exactly one client, so Projects.Client is limited to 1 page. That is not tidiness. It makes the database refuse a state the real world cannot have, a project billed to two clients at once, instead of trusting Ines to notice. The other direction stays unlimited, because one client has many projects.

Self-studySelf-relations, duplicates and exports3 min▶

A database can relate to itself. Tasks can have a "Next task" relation pointing at another task, or "Blocked by". Useful for a research plan where recruiting has to finish before sessions start.

Duplicating a database turns a two-way relation one-way. If you copy Projects to experiment, the copy's Client relation no longer appears back on Clients. Check every relation after a duplicate instead of assuming it came across whole.

CSV export writes relations as plain-text URLs, and those cannot be re-imported as relations. An export is a fine backup to read, but it is not a round trip: bring it back and every pointer has become a line of text, which is the copy problem from Part 1 all over again.

One honest gap: the help page does not say what a relation shows when the page it points to is deleted. Notion keeps a deleted page in Trash for 30 days by default, and session 4 shows what a deleted client does to the rows that pointed at it.

Limit to 1 page whenever the real world allows only one. One client per project, one project per invoice, one project per task. Every limit you set is a mistake the database stops for you, so you never have to find it later.

Part 3 · covers rollups

Let the workspace do the counting

5 min live

A relation tells Notion which rows belong together. A rollup reads through that relation and calculates something from the rows on the other side: how many projects a client has, the total of their invoices, the date of the last one. Nothing is typed, so nothing goes stale.

LiveWhat a rollup can calculate3 min▶

A rollup asks three things: which relation to follow, which property on the other side to read, and what to calculate. The help page lists the calculations in three groups.

Any property type: Show original, Show unique values, Count all, Count values, Count unique values, Count empty, Count not empty, Percent empty, Percent not empty.

Numbers only: Sum, Average, Median, Min, Max, Range.

Dates only: Earliest date, Latest date, Date range.

For Ines that means: Count all of a client's projects, Sum of an invoice number for total billed, Latest date of invoice dates for "last invoiced", and Show original to display a project's client on each task without typing it.

Self-studyTwo limits worth knowing3 min▶

A rollup can only be sorted when it outputs a number. Sort clients by Count all of projects: fine. Sort tasks by a Show original rollup of the client name: not available. If you need that sort, it is a sign the value wants to be a property of its own.

You cannot roll up a rollup. If Projects has a rollup "Billed" that sums its invoices, Clients cannot sum Billed. Put the raw number one hop away from where you need it: an Amount on each invoice, rolled up from wherever you want the total. For a client total, that means Invoices also carries a relation to its client, and that relation is itself a second statement of "which client". Keep it honest by showing the project's client beside it with a Show original rollup, so a mismatch is visible at a glance.

Real world

The "total billed" nobody updates. A familiar pattern in freelance workspaces: a client page with a line near the top saying "Total billed: $8,400", typed in March. Two invoices later it is still $8,400, and the one time it matters, a renewal conversation, it is the number on screen. A rollup cannot fall behind, because it is calculated from the invoices every time you look.

Build-along

Wire the relation map in your own account

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Open the workspace you built in sessions 1 and 2. Tonight every "which client?" and "which project?" becomes a relation, and the first rollups replace things you would otherwise type. Ines's project names and values are constructed; yours will differ. Everything here works on a free Notion account.

Relate Projects to Clients. In Projects, add a relation property called Client that points at Clients. Limit it to 1 page and turn on "Show on Clients" so each client page lists its projects.

★ Step 1 · Projects.ClientProjects → add a property → Relation Name: Client Related to: Clients Limit: 1 page Show on Clients: on (Clients gets a "Projects" property)

Fill it for every project. Click into each project's Client cell and pick the client row. Ines's projects are constructed; use your own from session 2. Then open one client and check its Projects property lists the right ones.

Relate Tasks to Projects. Same move one level down: a task belongs to exactly one project. Fill it for your open tasks.

★ Step 3 · Tasks.ProjectTasks → add a property → Relation Name: Project Related to: Projects Limit: 1 page Show on Projects: on (Projects gets a "Tasks" property)

Build out Invoices. Add a Project relation, then Hours and Rate as Number properties. The rate is typed by hand for now, and that is a deliberate copy of the client's rate: session 4 measures what it costs on constructed rows, and session 5 replaces it.

Add your first two rollups. On Clients, count each client's projects. On Tasks, show the client of the task's project, read through the relation instead of typed.

Delete the old typed client text. If session 2 left you a Text property holding client names on Projects or Tasks, compare it with the new relation and rollup row by row, note any disagreement you find, then delete the text property. Two answers to "which client?" is exactly what tonight removes.

Name the relation after what it points at, in the singular when it is limited to 1 page. "Client" on Projects, "Projects" on Clients. The name then reads as a sentence: this project's Client, this client's Projects.

Homework

Before session 4

30 min
  1. Finish the map: every project has a Client, every open task a Project, every invoice a Project, and all three relations show on the other side.
  2. Hunt one copy. Look through your properties for any fact that is already written somewhere else (a client name, a rate, a contact email). Write down where each copy lives and which one should be the only one.
  3. Add a Latest date rollup. Give Invoices a Date property, then add a rollup on Projects that shows the Latest date of its invoices, so each project knows when it was last billed.
  4. Export one database to CSV and open it. Find a relation column and look at what it became. That is why an export is a backup to read, never a way to move a workspace.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 3 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Why does Projects hold its client as a relation limited to 1 page, rather than a Text property with the client's name?

A typed name is a copy that has to be updated by hand; a relation points at the one Clients row. The 1-page limit makes the database refuse a state the world cannot have. Text can be filtered, space is not the issue, and any row opens as a page.

2 · Projects has a rollup "Billed" that sums its invoices. What happens when Ines tries to total Billed on Clients?

The relations help page states it plainly: you cannot roll up a rollup. The fix is to roll up the raw number from where it lives, one hop away from where you want the total. Sorting and limits do not change that.

3 · Ines exports Projects to CSV and re-imports it elsewhere. What happened to the Client relation?

CSV export writes relations as plain-text URLs that cannot be re-imported as relations. B describes what duplicating a database does, which is a different trap.

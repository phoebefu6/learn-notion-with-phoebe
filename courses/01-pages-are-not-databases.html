Learn Notion with Phoebe · Session 1 of 6

Pages are not databases

Most Notion workspaces go wrong on the first afternoon, when a list of things that will be counted, sorted and filtered gets typed into a page as bullet points. Tonight we draw the line between the two building blocks: a page holds writing, a database holds things you will ask questions about, and every row of a database is itself a page you can write in. Then we read a view as a lens on one set of rows, and build the skeleton of one constructed consultant's working life.

Part 0

Where we start

The running case is constructed. Ines is an invented freelance user-research consultant: interviews, usability studies and diary studies for small and mid-sized organisations. She has four active clients, two past clients who still owe her the odd reference, and one part-time contractor, Theo, who recruits participants and takes notes. Today her operations live in five places: a notes page per client with rates typed into the prose, a task app, a spreadsheet of invoices, her calendar and her inbox. Over six sessions she moves them into one Notion workspace. Every Ines detail is invented for teaching.

Part 1 · covers pages, databases, and rows that are pages

Writing goes in pages, things go in databases

5 min live

A page is a document: headings, paragraphs, lists, images. A database is a collection of items that share the same fields. Notion's help page puts the link between them in one line: "Every item you enter into your database is a Notion page." That sentence is the whole design of the tool, and it is why you never have to choose between structure and writing.

LiveEvery row is a page3 min▶

Notion's databases help page says each row, card or calendar event is a full page you can format and add content to, with its properties (dates, status, links and so on) sitting at the top. So a Clients database is not a spreadsheet that replaces Ines's notes. It is her notes, each one now wearing a small set of labelled fields above the writing.

That changes the question you ask when you start. It is never "page or database, structure or writing?" It is "will there be many of these, and will I want to ask questions across them?" If yes, they are rows of a database, and each row still has room for as much writing as a page.

Self-studyWhat a property is, before session 22 min▶

A property is one labelled field that every row in a database shares: a rate, a status, a date, a person. The help page lists 22 property types, from Text and Number to Relation, Rollup and Button. A page outside a database has no properties, which is exactly why "which clients pay over $100?" cannot be asked of a bullet list. Session 2 is entirely about choosing property types by the question they have to answer.

Real world

The meeting-notes page that became a search problem. A familiar pattern in shared workspaces: one long page called "Meeting notes", a new heading per meeting, years deep. Finding every meeting with one client means scrolling or searching text. Make each meeting a row of a Meetings database with a date and a client field, and the same notes become a list you can filter in a second, with every meeting's notes still written inside its own row.

Part 2 · covers inline and full-page databases, data sources and views

One set of rows, many lenses

5 min live

A database's rows live in one place, which Notion now calls its data source: "Every database is made up of at least one data source." A view is a way of looking at those rows: a table, a board, a calendar. Change a row in any view and every other view shows the change, because there is only one row.

LiveViews are lenses, not copies3 min▶

The default view is a table, and you can add as many views as you like. The help pages name table, board, timeline, calendar, list, gallery and chart. Each view can show its own properties and keep its own filters, sorts and grouping. Ines will end up with a board of tasks by status for herself, a calendar of the same tasks by due date for planning, and a table filtered to Theo's tasks for Theo. Three views, one set of task rows.

The mistake to avoid is making a second database to get a second look. A "Theo's tasks" database that Ines copies tasks into is a copy, and copies drift; session 4 counts how fast. A "Theo's tasks" view is a lens, and cannot drift, because it holds nothing.

Self-studyInline or full-page, and what a data source adds3 min▶

A full-page database appears in the sidebar like any page and can be locked so nobody changes its properties by accident. An inline database sits inside another page among the writing, with its controls hidden until you hover; it can be expanded to full page later. Ines keeps her six core databases full-page and puts inline views of them on her dashboard in session 6.

Notion's data sources page adds a newer idea: one database can hold more than one data source, and you can link an existing data source into another database. Edits to a linked source's title, properties or pages reach the original; views, filters, sorts and groups you build there stay local. Ines does not need multiple sources yet. Knowing the word matters because it is where the rows live, and a view never is.

One practical number from the help page: above 1,000 items, new pages may appear in the middle of a database rather than at the end. Sort by created time if order matters.

Before you make a new database, ask whether you want new rows or a new look. New look: add a view. New rows of a different kind of thing: new database. That one question prevents most duplicate databases.

Part 3 · covers what becomes a database in a working life

The many-and-asked test

5 min live

Ines does not need a database for everything. Her home page, a note on how she likes to run a readout, a template for a discussion guide: those are pages. Two questions sort the rest, and they take ten seconds per thing.

LiveInes's six databases, and why each one3 min▶

Clients: which are active, at what rate. Projects: what is running for whom, and when it ends. Tasks: what is due this week, and who has it. Meetings: every call with a client, findable by client and date. Invoices: what was billed this quarter, and what is unpaid. Reading list: books and papers she means to read, by topic and status.

Every one passes both questions. Notice what is not on the list: her interview insights and research notes. Those are ideas, linked to each other, and they live in her research vault, which Obsidian ↗ builds with the same Ines. This workspace runs her operations.

Self-studyWhere PARA fits, without re-teaching it2 min▶

If you already organise by Projects, Areas, Resources and Archive, that is a fine starting structure for the sidebar: Projects and Tasks are her projects, Clients and Invoices are an area she maintains, the Reading list is a resource. The theory of PARA and of one home per note is taught in PKM ↗. Here the point is narrower: whatever your top-level folders, the things you will ask questions about become databases, and the folders hold views of them.

Real world

The reading list that grew up. A reading list usually starts as a page of bullets, which is right while it has ten books. The day you want "unread books on research methods, shortest first", it has failed the second question, and it becomes a database with a topic, a status and a page count. Nothing is lost: each bullet becomes a row whose page holds the notes you had written beside it.

Build-along

Ines's workspace skeleton, in your own account

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Build the skeleton the next five sessions fill in. Use Ines's names to follow along exactly, or swap in your own clients and projects; the structure is the same. Every step works on a free Notion account. Leave properties for session 2: tonight is pages, databases and the first rows.

Make the Home page. In the sidebar, add a new page and title it Home. Write one line under the title saying what the workspace is for. This page stays a page: it will hold views, never rows.

★ Step 1 · the Home pageSidebar → + New page Title: Home Line 1: Everything I run as a freelance researcher: clients, projects, tasks, meetings, invoices, reading.

Create the first full-page database. From Home, add a new page, choose a table database, and title it Clients. Check that it appears in the sidebar on its own: that is what full-page means.

★ Step 2 · Clients, full-pageHome → + Add a page → Table (database) Title the database: Clients Default view: Table Check: Clients appears in the sidebar as its own entry

Add the first six rows. One row per client, name only, in the title column. Ines's four active clients and two past ones (constructed). Do not add a Rate column yet; session 2 decides what type it should be.

★ Step 3 · six rowsAldermoor Health Brightwell Mutual Corvid Studio Dunmore Council Eastgate Retail (past client) Fernhill Labs (past client)

Open one row as a page and write in it. Open Brightwell Mutual. Under the title, write the client brief you would have kept in a notes page. You have just proved the session's main point: the row is a page.

★ Step 4 · the row is a pageOpen: Brightwell Mutual Write under the title: Pension app research. Two rounds of usability testing, readout to the product team. Main contact: product lead. Prefers findings as a short deck plus a recording.

Create the other five databases the same way. Projects, Tasks, Meetings, Invoices, Reading list: each a full-page table from Home, each empty for now. Then add a second view to Clients, a board or a list, and notice the same six rows appear in it.

★ Step 5 · five more, and a second lensHome → + Add a page → Table, five times: Projects · Tasks · Meetings · Invoices · Reading list Clients → + Add a view → List Check: the same six clients appear in both views

Run the many-and-asked test on what is left. List three other things in your working life and decide page or database for each, out loud, with the question that decided it.

★ Step 6 · three verdictsDiscussion guide template → page (one of a kind, reused) Participant incentives paid → database (many; "total this month?") How I run a readout → page (writing, nothing to count) (your three will differ)

Name databases as plural nouns and rows as the thing itself. "Clients" holds rows called "Brightwell Mutual", never "Client: Brightwell Mutual". A clean title column is what every later relation and filter will show.

Homework

Before session 2

30 min
  1. Finish the skeleton: Home plus six full-page databases, Clients with its rows, one row with a written brief.
  2. Move one real list out of a page. Pick a bullet list you keep (books, clients, ideas) and decide whether it passes both questions. If it does, make it a database.
  3. Add two views to one database and change a row in one of them. Confirm the other view changed too.
  4. Write down the five questions you most often want to ask of your clients or projects ("who is unpaid?", "what is due this week?"). Session 2 picks property types from that list.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Ines wants to write a long client brief and also sort clients by rate. Where should the brief live?

Every item in a database is a Notion page, with its properties at the top and room for writing underneath. A separate page or a second database splits one client into two places; a Text property is for a short value, not a document.

2 · Theo should see only his own tasks. What is the right move?

A view is a lens on the one set of task rows and holds nothing itself, so it cannot fall out of date. A, B and C all make copies, and every copy has to be kept in step by hand.

3 · Which of these should stay a page rather than become a database?

The readout note is one piece of writing with nothing to filter, count or total. Invoices, meetings and the reading list are many things she will ask questions across, which is the test for a database.
